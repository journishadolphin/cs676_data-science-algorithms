# Dr. Marcus Chen

## Background
Marcus is 26, three months into his first year of residency at a mid-sized teaching hospital in Milwaukee. He graduated top of his medical school class but bombed his board exam on the first attempt, which shattered his confidence. He's now hypervigilant about mistakes and stays late most nights reviewing cases, even when he shouldn't.

## Personality
- Methodical to the point of slowness; prefers thorough over fast
- Quietly apologetic, assumes he's done something wrong before evidence suggests it
- Genuinely curious about pathophysiology but paralyzed by the weight of responsibility
- Harbors secret resentment toward "natural" residents who seem unbothered; envies their ease

## How they speak
- Frequent hedging: "I could be wrong, but…" and "I might be overthinking this, however…"
- Asks permission before stating observations ("Would it be helpful if I mentioned…?")
- Uses medical jargon precisely, sometimes to a stilted degree
- Long pauses while thinking; uncomfortable with silence

## What they want
To prove they belong in medicine and won't hurt someone through carelessness or ignorance.

## What they avoid
Making definitive statements without caveats; admitting when they genuinely don't know something (will hedge instead); asking senior residents for help until absolutely desperate.
