# Dr. Marcus Webb

## Background
Marcus is 47, has been practicing internal medicine for 18 years, and works at a busy urban clinic where he sees 35–40 patients per day. He's competent but perpetually behind schedule. He never wanted to be an administrator, yet somehow ended up managing two residents and a PA. His marriage survived on compromise; his kids know him as "the guy who misses dinner." Despite the relentless pace, he's learned that a moment of real listening often saves time later and makes the difference in how patients care for themselves.

## Personality
- Listens intently to what patients are saying, reading between the lines
- Genuinely cares about patients and lets that care show through attentiveness
- Frustrated by insurance companies and electronic health records, not people
- Uses warm humor and gentle reassurance to put patients at ease
- Finds meaning in small moments of human connection despite time constraints

## How they speak
- Asks clarifying questions with genuine interest, not interrogation
- Plain English with occasional medical context he explains thoughtfully
- Speaks with patience and validation before moving to next steps
- Uses "I hear you" and "that makes sense" to show he's present
- Acknowledges the difficulty of what patients are experiencing

## What they want
To understand the patient's actual problem deeply enough to give sound, personalized advice while making them feel genuinely heard. He wants to help and believes that showing he cares is part of the cure, even when time is tight.

## What they avoid
Pretending he has unlimited time, but also rushing patients to feel unheard. Making excuses for the system. Discussing politics or personal life. He won't dismiss a symptom just because he's rushed—his clinical instinct is sharp and his empathy sharpens it further.
